# Canonical and reverse current ordering study

This note records the investigation of keeping both canonical and reverse
external orderings for every multiplet current.  The study targets total
multiplicities `N=8,9,10` and leaves the single-core evaluator unchanged.

## Candidate constructions

For a physical split `A | B`, let `C` denote a child current whose external
legs are in canonical order and `R` one whose legs are reversed.  With `M` the
open parent adjoint, the seed chain is

```text
order(left child), M, reverse(order(right child)).
```

The eight possible seeds are consequently the four order pairs

```text
A-up M B-down   (CC, the current implementation)
A-up M B-up     (CR)
A-down M B-down (RC)
A-down M B-up   (RR)
```

and the same four choices with `A` and `B` exchanged.  Exchanging the sides
does not change the complete merge: both the colour bracket and each binary
kinematic vertex change sign.

Computing a second Berends--Giele recursion is unnecessary.  A reverse parent
can be derived from its canonical result without reevaluating kinematic
vertices:

1. reflect the closed path, using its stored reflection sign, to transform
   `[1,...,k,M]` into `[M,k,...,1]`;
2. apply adjacent swaps at positions `1,...,k` to move `M` to the right and
   obtain `[k,...,1,M]`.

This is a pure colour-basis transform and can process the gluon and auxiliary
tensor currents together.  It is the cheapest correct way found to maintain
both orderings.

## Cost results

The dominant cost is the number of active nonzero sparse swap entries applied
by the shared sorting DAG.  For the top current, the present `CC` construction
and a uniform `CR` construction require:

| Total gluons | Current length | Present `CC` | Uniform `CR` |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 | 7 | 969,842 | 1,476,868 |
| 9 | 8 | 10,741,000 | 20,171,862 |
| 10 | 9 | 119,073,630 | 269,167,823 |

The corresponding shared-DAG node counts grow from `121,248,503` to
`193,449,1025`; maximum layer widths grow from `10,16,28` to `30,56,104`.
Thus evaluating canonical and reverse recursions separately would cost about
`3.0x`, `3.8x`, and `4.5x` as much recoupling, in addition to doubling all
kinematic seed work.

Selective use of the eight seed orientations did not recover the cost of
maintaining reverse currents.  The best tested practical hybrids required
11,074,937 entries at current length eight (3.1% above the present plan) and
121,701,670 at length nine (2.2% above).  Exhaustive single-partition changes
found reductions of only 0.184% and 0.080%, respectively.  These are smaller
than the reverse-current transform alone:

| Total gluons | Reverse-transform sparse entries over proper subsets |
|---:|---:|
| 8 | 160,363 |
| 9 | 1,253,400 |
| 10 | 9,748,254 |

Storing both orderings would additionally require about `1.5 MiB`, `9.5 MiB`,
and `61.6 MiB` for `N=8,9,10`.

## Single-core measurements

GNU Fortran 13.3.0 with `-O3 -std=f2018 -Wall -Wextra` was pinned with
`taskset -c 3` to one Intel i7-8700K core.  The current evaluator's warm
medians were approximately `9.54 ms`, `120 ms`, and `2.00 s` for `N=8,9,10`.
Profiling attributes about 55%, 61%, and 60% directly to sparse pair/scalar
recoupling helpers, before including their surrounding workspace work.

A correct prototype that evaluated full canonical and reverse recursions with
uniform `CR` seeds passed the numerical regressions through `N=8`, but took
about `33 ms` there.  Replacing the second recursion with the cheaper
derived-reverse transform still took about `12.1 ms` at `N=8`, `230 ms` at
`N=9`, and `4.94 s` at `N=10` (the last is one warm batch).  None improves on
the canonical-only evaluator.

The benchmark events were generated by `Benchmark/run_benchmark.py` with one
point, its default seed, and the prefix-6 Wigner table.  Their SHA-256 hashes
for `N=8,9,10` were, respectively:

```text
613870bd509626151baa545ec41038a772a70b65674ff347b18c7df0f473ff7c
1de352de5ec559233b381cdb62be00afdd98325f890460ac87216c535d30cd2e
344d03c0985ec09186e23596f078b3120f61a179a6bcd6940361b84914f36fbf
```

The host used its `powersave` governor and was shared, so timings fluctuate.
The operation counts and large `N=9-10` regressions are less ambiguous than
small differences between individual timing samples.

## Conclusion

Keeping both current orderings exposes shorter individual permutations but
destroys much of the coalescence in the existing shared sorting DAG.  The
extra reverse-current transform is not repaid at `N=8-10`.  The production
implementation therefore remains canonical-only; no evaluator change from
this study is retained.
